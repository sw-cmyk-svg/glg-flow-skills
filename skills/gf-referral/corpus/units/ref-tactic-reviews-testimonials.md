**Tactic — turn promoters into reviews, testimonials, and case studies — but stay inside the hard legal line: real, disclosed, not conditioned on being positive.**

Advocacy that others can see — reviews, testimonials, case studies, G2/app-store ratings — is a compounding
acquisition asset: it works as third-party proof for strangers long after it's created. Harvest it from
genuine promoters at their advocacy moment. The hard constraints come straight from the **FTC Consumer
Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective Oct 2024)**, and they are not optional:

- **No fake reviews** — don't write, buy, sell, or commission reviews from people who didn't use the
  product (AI-generated fake reviews explicitly included).
- **No conditioning the reward on positivity** — you may invite or even incentivise *a review*, but you may
  **not** make a reward contingent on it being **positive** (or on a specific star rating).
- **Disclose material connections** — employees, insiders, or anyone given an incentive must clearly
  disclose the connection; undisclosed insider reviews are barred.
- **No suppression / fakery** — don't hide negative reviews, present a site you control as "independent," or
  buy fake followers/views.

**How to apply.** Ask satisfied promoters for honest reviews; make the ask easy (direct links to the review
surface) and time it to the value moment. If you incentivise participation, reward **leaving a review**, not
a **good** one, and keep it modest. Require clear disclosure for any incentivised or insider testimonial.
Respond to negatives publicly rather than hiding them.

**When it applies.** Building a reviews/testimonials/social-proof engine off an advocacy or NPS program;
reviewing an existing one for legal exposure.

**Caution (ethics):** this is a **legal line, not just an ethical one** — knowing violations carry FTC civil
penalties, and the rule is being enforced (warning letters issued 2025). The ethos is authentic advocacy:
real customers, real experiences, honest disclosure. Non-US clients face their own equivalents (e.g. UK
CMA / EU UCPD on fake reviews) — flag for the jurisdiction. Pairs with
[[ref-framework-nps-promoter-advocacy]] (who to ask) and [[ref-tactic-invite-anti-patterns]] (the same
no-fakery ethos on the invite side).
