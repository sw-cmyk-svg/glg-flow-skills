**Principle — put the invite where the value is felt, not on a page nobody visits; the surface drives sharing more than the size of the reward.**

A "Refer a friend" link buried in settings gets almost no traffic no matter how good the offer. Invites
come from **contextual prompts at the right moment**: just after the user got a result, at natural
collaboration points (sharing a document, inviting a teammate, needing a second person to complete a task),
in empty states that are better with others, and after a positive signal (a good rating, a renewal). These
placements also **shorten viral cycle time** — the gap between feeling value and sending an invite — which
compounds harder than a bigger reward (see the k-factor framework). The best surfaces ride the product's
inherent collaboration, so the invite is useful to the user, not just to you.

**How to apply.** Inventory the product's value and collaboration moments and place a light invite prompt at
each, instead of relying on one standing referral page. Trigger the ask on positive events (completed
milestone, high NPS, repeat use). Keep each prompt small and skippable. Compare invite-per-surface to find
the one or two placements that produce most invites and concentrate there.

**When it applies.** Deciding where invite prompts live; a program with a generous reward but almost no
invites sent (usually a placement problem, not an offer problem).

**Caution (ethics):** a contextual ask must stay skippable and must not block the user's own progress
("invite to continue" is a dark pattern — [[ref-tactic-invite-anti-patterns]]). Timing the ask to a value
moment is legitimate; manufacturing false urgency around it is not. Pairs with
[[ref-tactic-reduce-invite-friction]] and [[ref-principle-advocacy-downstream-of-retention]] (the user must
actually have felt value first).
