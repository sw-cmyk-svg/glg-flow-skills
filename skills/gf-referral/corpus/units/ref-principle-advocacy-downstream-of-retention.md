**Principle — advocacy is downstream of retention: only users who get sustained value refer, so a flat referral program is often a satisfaction problem, not a mechanic problem.**

Referrals are produced by **happy, retained users** — people recommend things they still use and value. This
makes the referral loop dependent on Retention's output: pour acquisition into a product users churn out of,
and there is no one left to advocate. So when a referral program underperforms, the **first diagnostic is
not the mechanic but the fuel**: are users retaining and getting value? If engagement and retention are
weak, a bigger reward won't fix it — you'd just be paying unhappy users to send friends into a product that
won't keep them (and, per the referred-value research, those referred users churn once the referrer does).
The honest move is to route the root cause to Retention/Activation, not to inflate the bounty.

**How to apply.** Before tuning a referral program, check referrer health: are the users you're asking to
refer actually retained and active? Segment the ask to engaged/retained cohorts and promoters, not the whole
base. If referrals are flat *and* retention is weak, say so and route the value gap to Retention — Referral
owns the loop mechanic, not the underlying satisfaction. If retention is healthy but referrals are still
flat, *then* the problem is in the mechanic (invite flow, reward, placement).

**When it applies.** Diagnosing an underperforming referral program; deciding whether the fix is Referral's
or Retention's; choosing which cohort to ask.

**Caution (honesty).** This is the **Referral↔Retention seam** — don't absorb a retention problem into a
referral fix, and don't let a founder paper over churn with a bigger bribe. Needs retention/engagement data
by cohort to act on. Pairs with [[ref-framework-nps-promoter-advocacy]] (promoters are the retained-happy
users) and [[ref-principle-referred-users-more-valuable]] (the referrer-dependence finding).
