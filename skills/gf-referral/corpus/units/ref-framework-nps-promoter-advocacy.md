**Framework — use NPS to find your likely advocates and ask them at that moment; don't treat the score as a growth forecast.**

Net Promoter asks one question — *how likely are you to recommend us?* (0–10) — and splits respondents into
**promoters (9–10)**, **passives (7–8)**, and **detractors (0–6)**. Its real referral value is not the
headline score but the **segmentation and the timing signal**: a user who just answered 9–10 is, right now,
the most willing to advocate they will ever be — the ideal moment to invite a referral or ask for a review.
Route detractors to support/recovery instead. The score is a useful *pulse* and a *who-to-ask* filter, not
a law: research after Reichheld's 2003 article challenged the claim that NPS predicts growth better than
other satisfaction measures, so treat the number as directional.

**How to apply.** Trigger the referral/advocacy ask off a fresh promoter response (survey, in-product
rating, a renewal) rather than a cold standing page — advocacy asks convert far better at the promoter
moment. Use detractor responses to fix problems, not to solicit referrals. Track NPS as one satisfaction
signal among several (alongside actual retention), not as the target you optimise.

**When it applies.** Deciding whom to ask for referrals/reviews and when; building the trigger that connects
satisfaction to the advocacy ask.

**Caution (honesty).** NPS is **directional, self-reported, and gameable** (pestering for 9s corrupts it);
it is not a growth KPI and has **no spec id** — don't present it as a forecast. Actual behaviour (did happy
users refer, did referred users stick) beats the stated score. Pairs with
[[ref-principle-advocacy-downstream-of-retention]] (promoters come from retained, satisfied users) and
[[ref-tactic-reviews-testimonials]] (what to do with a promoter).
