**Framework — there are three kinds of virality, and they are not interchangeable: pick the one your product can actually support.**

- **Inherent virality** — the product spreads *because of how it is used*, with no reward and no bolted-on
  invite. Two modern shapes: **collaboration virality**, where using it means pulling other people in (a
  shared document, a team workspace, a payment that needs a recipient, a calendar invite); and **output /
  content virality**, where the product's *output* reaches non-users as a side effect of normal use (a Loom
  recording in an inbox, a shared Figma file, a Canva design, an AI-generated artifact). In both, the spread
  rides real usage, so cycle time is short and the users it brings are well-matched. This is the strongest,
  most durable kind — but you can't bolt it on; it comes from the product's shape, and it is where modern
  virality mostly lives (see [[ref-principle-modern-viral-surfaces]]).
- **Word-of-mouth** — users tell others because the product is remarkable, with no mechanism and no reward.
  Unincentivized and trustworthy, but you influence it only indirectly (make the product worth talking
  about, make sharing easy); you can't engineer it to a target.
- **Incentivized / "artificial" virality** — a referral program layered on top: rewards for inviting
  (two-sided credits, cash, status). Buildable and measurable, but the weakest and most gameable — it can
  attract reward-seekers rather than good-fit users, and it stops when the reward stops.

**How to apply.** Diagnose which type is realistic *before* spending on a program. If the product has
inherent virality, invest in that loop — collaboration or output (fastest cycle, best cohorts) — before any
paid-reward scheme. If it doesn't, a referral program is a legitimate amplifier — but size expectations to "artificial"
virality (modest, reward-dependent), and watch referred-cohort quality. Don't try to buy inherent virality
with incentives; a bribe can't manufacture a reason for the product to spread through normal use.

**When it applies.** Choosing whether/what loop to build; explaining why a referral program underperforms a
competitor's "automatic" growth (they likely have inherent virality you don't).

**Caution (honesty).** This taxonomy is a **concept** for reasoning, not a measured model. Incentivized
virality is where ethics bite hardest — rewards must not become coerced invites or spam (see the invite
anti-patterns in wave 2). Pairs with [[ref-framework-viral-loop-k-factor]] (the math under any type) and
[[ref-framework-two-sided-referral-program]] (designing the incentivized kind well).
