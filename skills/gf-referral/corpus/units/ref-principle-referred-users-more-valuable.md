**Principle — referred customers tend to be more valuable and cheaper to acquire — but the advantage is conditional, not automatic.**

The grounded finding: referred customers are worth **at least ~16% more** than otherwise-comparable non-
referred customers (matched on demographics and acquisition timing), and cost less to acquire — the headline
case for building a referral loop at all. Two honest qualifiers from the same research program, both of which
change how you use the number: the **margin advantage erodes over time** (referred users aren't permanently
higher-spending), while the **retention advantage holds** — and that retention edge exists **only as long as
the referrer stays** (referred users churn more once their referrer leaves). The mechanisms are **better
matching** (friends refer good-fit friends) and **social enrichment** (the shared tie deepens use). So the
value comes from *good-fit users brought by happy users*, not from the reward — which is why targeting
quality and keeping referrers happy matters more than a bigger bounty.

**How to apply.** Use "referred users are more valuable" to justify the program and to prefer referral over
raw paid volume — but verify it **in the client's own data by cohort** rather than importing the 16%, and
watch for the erosion and referrer-dependence (losing a connected referrer can take their referred users
too). Design for matching quality (so friends bring good-fit friends) and for referrer retention, not just
invite volume.

**When it applies.** Making the business case for a referral program; valuing referred cohorts; deciding
whether referral beats paid for a given segment.

**Caution (honesty).** The ~16% is **one German retail-bank study (2011)** — directional, not a universal
SaaS number; **do not quote it as a client's expected lift** (the recency/transfer rule). It needs the
client's own cohort data to confirm. Pairs with [[ref-principle-cac-via-referral]] (the cost side) and the
Retention seam [[ref-principle-advocacy-downstream-of-retention]] (happy referrers are the source).
