**Principle — pay the reward on the invitee's qualified action, not on their signup, and ask for the referral at the moment the user just felt value.**

Two timing decisions make or break a program. **When you ask:** a referral request lands best right after a
user experiences value — completing a project, hitting a result, the "aha" moment — not on a cold "refer a
friend" page they never visit. **When you pay:** gate the reward on the referred user doing something
*qualified* (activating, or making a first payment), not merely creating an account. Rewarding on signup
invites fraud and floods the funnel with reward-seekers who never activate; rewarding on a real downstream
action aligns the incentive with bringing in *good-fit* users and makes the program's economics honest
(you pay only when you got a user who matters).

**How to apply.** Place the invite prompt contextually at value moments in the product, not only in
settings. Define the reward trigger as the referred user's **activation event or first payment**, and say
so in the program terms. This naturally throttles fraud and keeps referred-cohort quality comparable to
organic — the thing you actually want. If the client currently rewards on signup, that is usually the first
fix.

**When it applies.** Designing the reward trigger and the invite placement; diagnosing a program with high
invites but poor referred-cohort activation, or a fraud/gaming problem.

**Caution (ethics):** the ask must be genuine, not coercive — never gate the user's own progress behind
inviting others ("invite 3 friends to continue"); that is a dark pattern. Rewarding on a qualified action
is also the main anti-fraud control, but not the only one (see the fraud-controls unit in wave 2). Needs
**downstream cohort data** (did referred users activate?) many clients don't track cleanly — say what's
needed. Pairs with [[ref-framework-two-sided-referral-program]]; mirrors the Acquisition cohort-quality
read [[acq-principle-cohort-quality-vs-vanity-volume]].
