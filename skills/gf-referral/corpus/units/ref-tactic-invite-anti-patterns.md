**Anti-pattern unit — the invite tactics that win short-term and poison the well: documented so they are recognised and refused, never prescribed.**

Invite flows are where referral growth most often turns predatory, because they reach **other people's
personal data**. The anti-patterns:

- **Forced or pre-checked contact imports** — "connect your address book to continue," or a consent box
  pre-ticked to invite everyone. Imports contacts without genuine opt-in and blasts people who never agreed.
- **Address-book mass-mailing / spam** — sending invites to a user's entire contact list, especially with
  the message dressed up as a personal note the user didn't write. Breaches anti-spam law (CAN-SPAM) and
  platform policy, and burns the user's trust with their own contacts.
- **Coercive invite walls** — gating the user's own progress or features behind inviting N friends ("invite
  3 to unlock"). Hostage-taking, not advocacy.
- **Fake-identity invites** — sending from the user's name/email so the invite looks hand-written when it
  wasn't.

Each buys a short spike and costs long-term trust, deliverability, and often legal exposure.

**How to apply (the honest alternatives).** Make every invite an **explicit, opt-in, user-initiated** act:
show exactly who will be contacted and with what, before anything sends. Never store a non-user's contact
data beyond the single invite. Default to shareable links the user controls over contact-list access.
Respect consent and the data boundary (HR2): no scraping, no silent imports, no retained address books.

**When it applies.** Reviewing any invite mechanic; pushing back when a growth target tempts a contact-list
blast; a client asking to "just email everyone's contacts."

**Caution (ethics):** this is the hard line of Referral's ethics edge — **authentic advocacy, never
weaponizing a user's relationships**. Several of these breach anti-spam, consumer-protection, or data-
protection law (GDPR consent for processing contacts). Regulated archetypes raise the stakes further. Pairs
with [[ref-tactic-reduce-invite-friction]] (low friction ≠ coercion) and the privacy stance in the charter.
