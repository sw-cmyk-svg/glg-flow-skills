**Principle — reward in the product's own currency where you can; cash attracts people who want the cash, not the product.**

The *type* of reward selects *who* refers and *who* they bring. A reward paid in the product's own value —
storage, usage credits, extra seats, feature access — does three things cash can't: it costs the company
little at the margin, it deepens the referrer's engagement (they use the product more), and it draws
invitees who actually want the product (you can only spend the reward *inside* it). Cash and gift cards are
universal and easy to understand, but they attract **reward-seekers** who refer for the payout and bring
others who do the same — poorer-fit, lower-retention cohorts — and they cost real money. PayPal could use
cash honestly because money *was* its product; Dropbox used space because storage was. Match the reward to
what your product makes cheap and meaningful.

**How to apply.** Prefer an in-product reward tied to continued use. Reserve cash for products where money
or near-money is the value (fintech, marketplaces), or where no in-product currency exists — and if you use
cash, watch referred-cohort quality closely for mercenary behaviour. Whatever the currency, size it against
qualified CAC and gate it on a real action (see the timing unit).

**When it applies.** Choosing the reward currency; diagnosing a cash program that brings high volume but
low-retention referred cohorts.

**Caution (ethics):** large cash incentives are the most fraud- and mercenary-prone — document, don't
prescribe, schemes that reward raw signups for cash. For regulated archetypes (bank/wallet, betting) a cash
or bonus reward is a **financial promotion / regulated incentive** (responsible-use, no targeting
minors/vulnerable) — flag once, then design concretely; shape the corpus before serving that industry
(P7). Pairs with [[ref-framework-two-sided-referral-program]] and [[ref-principle-reward-timing-value-moment]].
