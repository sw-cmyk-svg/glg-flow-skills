**Principle — the viral *math* is timeless, but the *surfaces* have moved: the 2009 invite-loop playbook is largely closed, and the modern engines are output/content virality and network effects — not contact-import referral hacks.**

The k-factor formula ([[ref-framework-viral-loop-k-factor]]) still holds, but *where* virality happens has
changed, and a loop designed for a 2009 world will underperform:

- **The classic channels closed.** Platform-API virality (the open-graph era) and address-book / email-
  invite loops are mostly dead or now illegal — throttled by the platforms and constrained by anti-spam and
  data-protection law (the same law behind the invite anti-patterns and the FTC reviews rule). Chasing those
  is a dead end.
- **Output / content virality is the dominant modern *inherent* loop.** The strongest loops today put the
  **product's output in front of non-users as a side effect of normal use**: a shared Figma file, a Loom
  recording in an inbox, a Calendly/Typeform link, a Canva design, an AI-generated artifact. No reward, no
  invite friction — the work itself is the distribution, which gives a short cycle time and good-fit reach.
  This is the modern face of "inherent virality."
- **Aim for a network effect, not just a loop.** Chen's *Cold Start Problem* (2021) argues the durable
  engine is a **network effect** reached via a dense enough **atomic network** — a loop that also makes the
  product *better as more people join* compounds and defends, where a bare referral loop decays. Network
  effects also hit **anti-network effects at scale** (saturation, congestion, quality drop) — the modern
  version of loop decay.

**How to apply.** Before designing a referral *program*, ask whether the product can carry an **output or
content loop** built into normal use — that usually beats a bolted-on invite-reward scheme (faster cycle,
better cohorts, no reward cost). For products with a real network, design toward the atomic network and the
network effect, not just a one-shot k. Treat an incentivised referral program as the *amplifier* it is
([[ref-principle-most-loops-decay-k-below-1]]), layered on a product that already has a reason to spread.

**When it applies.** Choosing what kind of loop to build in a 2024-era product; a founder asking to recreate
a Dropbox/PayPal-style referral growth spurt; assessing whether virality is even available to this product.

**Caution (honesty).** The named examples (Figma, Loom, Canva, AI products) illustrate the **pattern** — the
circulating per-company virality percentages are vendor-blog estimates and are **not** cited as figures.
Output virality isn't available to every product (a single-player tool with private output can't spread this
way — say so). Network effects are over-claimed in pitches; a shareable output is not automatically a
network effect. Pairs with [[ref-framework-virality-types]] (output virality is a modern *inherent* type)
and [[ref-principle-cycle-time-and-compounding]] (output loops shorten ct).
