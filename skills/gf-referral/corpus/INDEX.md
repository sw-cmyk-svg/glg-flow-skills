**Curation principle — persuasion & ethics (inherited).** Persuasion tactics are allowed, but every
persuasion-adjacent unit MUST carry an explicit `Caution (ethics):` line. Referral's ethics edge is
**authentic advocacy — never weaponizing a user's relationships**: forced / pre-checked contact-list
imports and address-book mass-mailing (spam), undisclosed paid advocacy, fake reviews / astroturfing (an
FTC / consumer-protection breach), coercive invite walls, and reward-fraud facilitation are dark patterns —
documented as anti-patterns, never prescribed. **Privacy is first-class** (invite flows touch personal
data — consent + HR2). Regulated archetypes (bank/wallet, betting) get a financial-promotion /
responsible-use compliance caution, not a ban.

## Six tactic families (target ≥3 each)

1. **viral-loops** — loop type and engineering: inherent/collaboration virality vs word-of-mouth vs
   incentivized; the k-factor and viral-cycle-time mechanics; why most loops sit k<1 and decay.
2. **referral-program-design** — program structure and incentive: one- vs two-sided, reward type /
   magnitude / timing.
3. **invite-mechanics** — the invite flow and sharing surfaces; friction reduction without spam or coerced
   contact imports.
4. **incentive-economics** — reward economics: CAC-via-referral vs paid, sustainability, fraud / gaming
   limits, referred-user value.
5. **advocacy-nps** — turning satisfied retained users into advocates: NPS / promoters, reviews /
   testimonials, timing the ask; advocacy is downstream of retention.
6. **referral-measurement** — measuring virality honestly: k-factor confounds, self-selection, referred-vs-
   organic attribution, incrementality over dashboards.

**Recency top-up (2026-11-19, founder's challenge on the Skok anchor).** The founder flagged that anchoring
viral-loops on Skok (2009) risks a dated worldview given how much virality has shifted recently. Resolution:
Skok stays as the **concept anchor for the k-factor *math*** (K=i×c, K^(t/ct) is timeless arithmetic, cites
no Skok *figures* — within the old-refs-for-concepts rule), but a **modern-surfaces unit** was added and
`virality-types` refreshed so the family is *timeless-math + current-surface*, not 2009-only. The new unit
(`ref-principle-modern-viral-surfaces`) is grounded on **Chen's *The Cold Start Problem* (2021)** — network
effects / the atomic network as the durable modern engine, anti-network effects as modern loop-decay — plus
the **output/content-virality pattern** (the product's output is the distribution: Figma / Loom / Canva /
AI artifacts), with the classic 2009 channels (platform APIs, contact-import, email-invite spam) noted as
largely closed by platforms + anti-spam/data-protection law. Per the recency discipline, the **named
companies illustrate the pattern only** — the circulating per-company virality percentages (e.g. "87%
invited by colleagues", "k 0.3–0.7") are vendor-blog estimates and were **deliberately not cited**.
`virality-types` now folds **output/content virality** into "inherent virality" alongside collaboration.

**Wave-3 verification notes (per founder's standing ask).** advocacy-nps: NPS is deliberately **concept-
grounded** (Reichheld, *HBR* 2003) with its honesty qualifier baked in (post-2003 research challenged NPS as
a superior growth predictor — it identifies likely advocates and the moment to ask, it does not forecast
growth); the reviews/testimonials unit is anchored to a **recent primary legal source** — the **FTC Consumer
Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465**, verified at ftc.gov (final rule Aug 2024, **effective Oct
21 2024**): bans fake/AI reviews, buying/selling them, undisclosed insider/material-connection reviews,
**rewards conditioned on positive sentiment**, and review suppression, with civil penalties (warning letters
issued Dec 2025) — so the unit's line is "incentivise *a review*, never a *positive* one; disclose every
material connection"; advocacy-downstream-of-retention is the Referral↔Retention seam. referral-measurement:
self-selection-confound is the stage's core honesty unit (attributed referrals overstate incremental users —
your best users would have referred anyway); incrementality-over-attribution mirrors the Acquisition
attribution-limits unit (holdout > dashboard); cycle-time-and-compounding is the measurement corollary of
Skok's primary K^(t/ct) (instrument ct, not just k). **Corpus now robust (18 units, all six families ≥3) —
ready for the specialist anatomy (playbook + I/O contracts + assurance) and the D-03 proof.**

**Wave-2 verification notes (per founder's standing ask — recent data only for figures).** The only hard
figure in wave 2 is read at **primary**: **Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte, "Referral Programs and Customer
Value," *Journal of Marketing* 75 (2011): 46–59** (authors' Wharton hosting) — referred customers worth
**≥16%** more than otherwise-comparable non-referred, from **one German retail bank** (2011 MSI/Root Award).
Qualified by the same group's follow-up (**JMR 2015**): the margin advantage **erodes** while the retention
advantage **holds — but only while the referrer stays** (mechanisms: better matching + social enrichment).
The 16% is used as a **concept** (referred users tend to be more valuable and cheaper), explicitly flagged
as single-study and **not to be quoted as a client's expected lift** — confirm in the client's own cohort
data. Everything else in wave 2 (invite friction, share surfaces, fraud controls, CAC-via-referral) is
established practice with no live figures to verify; the invite-anti-patterns unit is the ethics/privacy
anchor (spam / forced contact imports / coercive walls / fake-identity invites — documented, never
prescribed; several breach anti-spam / consumer-protection / data-protection law). Nothing fabricated.

**Wave-1 verification notes (per founder's standing ask — recent data only for figures).** The one
load-bearing framework (**k-factor + cycle time**) is read at **primary** — Skok's 2009 essay
(forentrepreneurs.com): **K = invitations × conversion rate**, growth ∝ **K^(t/ct)** so reducing cycle time
beats raising K, **"The Viral Coefficient must be greater than 1 to have viral growth"** and **"only a very
small number of companies actually achieve true viral growth,"** plus the **hybrid/amplifier** model (cover
the shortfall with paid/SEO) and **"virality is not a marketing strategy that can be executed by the
marketing department."** The **case figures are deliberately concept-only**: Dropbox's "3900% in 15 months"
and "~35% of signups," and PayPal's "$20→$10→$5 / 7–10% daily / ~$60–70M," are **old (2000, 2008–10) and
reach us only through varying secondary blogs** — used to illustrate the *mechanism* (two-sided,
reward-matched-to-product, step-down as network value grows), with the numbers flagged historical and
non-transferable, never quoted to a client as a target. Nothing fabricated.
