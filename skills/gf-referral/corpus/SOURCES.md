# Referral corpus — sources

**Business model:** `plg-saas` (proof archetype). Same curation rules as the prior four specialists:
GL-authored distilled note + citation (transformative / copyright-safe, never reproduces source text),
I-draft-you-vet, verification-as-drafting, and the persuasion-ethics principle (every persuasion-adjacent
unit carries a mandatory `Caution (ethics):` line; dark patterns documented only as anti-patterns).

**Recency discipline (founder's standing ask).** Referral's canonical cases are **old** — PayPal (2000),
Dropbox (2008–2010), Hotmail (1996). Per the rule, these are used as **concepts**, never as live figures a
founder would act on; their famous numbers are flagged as historical, secondary-sourced, and
non-transferable. k-factor benchmarks are **genuinely scarce and soft** — stated as such, never faked.

## In-hand (carry over from prior specialists)

- **Brian Balfour / Reforge — growth loops** (loops vs funnels, compounding). The referral loop is a
  special case of the growth loop. Acquisition already cites `acq-principle-owned-vs-rented-channels`.
- **Andrew Chen** — viral growth, k-factor framing, and the "law of shitty clickthroughs" (which applies
  to invite channels too: invite acceptance decays as a channel saturates a user's network). Acquisition
  cites `acq-principle-channel-saturation`.
- **The book** — Shotton & Flicker, *Hacking the Human Mind* (Macmillan, 2025). Referral-relevant
  chapters: **social proof** (advocacy / testimonials) and **reciprocity** (two-sided rewards), each with
  its ethics caution.

## To mine (public, SaaS-native)

- **David Skok, "Lessons Learned — Viral Marketing"** (forentrepreneurs.com, 2009) — **the k-factor
  formula** (K = invites × conversion), **viral cycle time** (ct) and why reducing ct beats raising K
  (K^(t/ct)), K>1 is rare, and the **hybrid / amplifier** model. **Primary, read.** *(viral-loops)*
- **Andrew Chen** — types of virality, "what's your viral coefficient," why most products sit k<1.
  *(viral-loops, referral-measurement)*
- **Dropbox** (Drew Houston, 2010 Startup Lessons Learned talk; *Hacking Growth*, Sean Ellis) — the
  **two-sided space reward** referral program; the famous 3900%/15-month and ~35%-of-signups figures —
  **concept + historical figures, flagged secondary.** *(referral-program-design)*
- **PayPal** (Thiel, *Zero to One*; Jarvis / aakashg teardowns) — the **two-sided cash bonus**
  ($20→$10→$5 step-down), ~7–10% daily growth, ~$60–70M total — **concept + historical, flagged.**
  *(referral-program-design, incentive-economics)*
- **Van den Bulte, Schmitt & Skiera, "Referral Programs and Customer Value"** (*Journal of Marketing*,
  2011) — the finding that **referred customers are more valuable and cheaper to acquire** (higher margin,
  higher retention). **Foundational concept; the exact % is one 2011 bank study, flagged.**
  *(incentive-economics)*
- **Reichheld, "The One Number You Need to Grow"** (*HBR*, 2003) — **NPS** as a promoter-identification
  tool; used as a **concept** with its modern critique (NPS is directional, not a growth law).
  *(advocacy-nps)*
- **FTC** — the **2024 Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials** (16 CFR Part 465, effective
  Oct 2024) — bans fake/undisclosed reviews. **Recent, primary, citable** as the hard line under the
  advocacy ethics caution. *(advocacy-nps)*

## Benchmark priors to anchor (verify at source; most are soft)

- **Viral coefficient (k-factor)** — **k > 1 = self-sustaining virality; the large majority of products
  sit k < 1** and use the loop as an amplifier, not an engine (Skok, Chen). **Soft — no reliable public
  median.** *(viral-loops)*
- **Viral cycle time** — reducing ct compounds harder than raising k (Skok's K^(t/ct)). *(viral-loops)*
- **Referral program participation** — share of users who ever refer is **typically a minority** (often
  cited single-digit-to-~10% but highly program-dependent) — **soft, flag as directional.**
  *(referral-measurement)*
- **Referred-customer value** — referred customers tend to retain better and cost less than the average
  (Van den Bulte 2011, one bank study) — **concept, not a universal number.** *(incentive-economics)*
