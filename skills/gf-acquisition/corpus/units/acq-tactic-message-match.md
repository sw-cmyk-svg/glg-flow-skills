**Tactic — the landing page must keep the promise the ad made (message match).**

A visitor clicks an ad because of a specific promise; the page they land on has to deliver *that*
promise, in the same words and framing. When the ad and the page match — headline, offer, and visual
"scent" — the visitor stays and converts; when the page is generic or talks about something else, they
bounce even though the traffic was well-targeted. Message match is one of the highest-leverage fixes
for a surface that gets clicks but few signups, because the traffic is already interested — the page is
just failing to confirm it's in the right place.

**How to apply.** For each ad or campaign, make the landing page's headline echo the ad's core promise
and audience, not a one-size-fits-all homepage. Match the specific offer (if the ad said "free
30-day", the page says "free 30-day"), keep the primary call-to-action singular and obvious, and strip
anything that contradicts the promise. Dedicated landing pages per campaign usually beat routing paid
traffic to the homepage. This directly targets `signup_rate`.

**When it applies.** Any paid or campaign traffic with decent volume but low visitor→signup; a mismatch
between ad creative and page is the first thing to check (alongside positioning / message-market fit).

**Caution (ethics).** Match the promise honestly — the page must deliver what the ad implied. Making
the ad promise more than the product gives (bait-and-switch), or matching with fake urgency/scarcity
the product doesn't have, is a deceptive-marketing anti-pattern, not a conversion tactic. Pairs with
[[acq-principle-message-market-fit]] (the message itself) and [[acq-tactic-reduce-signup-friction]]
(the step after the click).
