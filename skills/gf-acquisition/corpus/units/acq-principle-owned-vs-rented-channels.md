**Principle — owned and earned channels compound and you keep the audience; paid is rented and stops when the spend stops.**

Acquisition channels split by who owns the audience. **Paid** (search, social, display) is *rented*:
it turns on instantly and scales with budget, but the moment you stop paying, the traffic stops, and
the cost per signup rises as you scale it (auction dynamics, saturation). **Owned / earned** channels
(SEO content, an email list, a community, product-led virality) are *assets*: they take longer to
build and don't scale on command, but they keep delivering signups after the work is done and you're
not re-paying for each visitor. A healthy acquisition mix usually pairs a rented channel for speed with
an owned channel that compounds, so growth isn't 100% hostage to ad spend.

**How to apply.** Classify a client's channels as rented vs owned, and check the balance. If growth is
entirely paid, flag the fragility (CAC rises, margins thin, a budget cut kills the pipeline) and point
to an owned channel worth seeding now so it can compound later. If the client has no runway for a slow
owned channel, say so — owned channels are an investment with a lag, not a quick fix.

**When it applies.** Channel-mix strategy, a client over-reliant on paid, or planning for durable
(not just fast) acquisition.

**Caution (honesty).** Owned channels have a **real lead time** (SEO and content can take months to
pay back) and aren't free — they cost time and content, just not per-click. Don't pitch "switch to
organic" as a cheap or fast replacement for paid; it's a different time horizon. CAC/channel economics
have no canonical spec id — flag definitions. Pairs with [[acq-principle-content-compounds]] and
[[acq-principle-channel-saturation]] (why the rented side decays).
