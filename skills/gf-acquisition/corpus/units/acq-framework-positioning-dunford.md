**Framework — positioning is the context you set so the right buyer instantly gets why your product matters. It comes before messaging and targeting.**

Positioning is the deliberate choice of the market context in which you place your product, so its
value is obvious to the customers best suited to it. It is the *input* to everything downstream —
message, channel, and targeting all flow from it, so weak positioning can't be fixed with better ad
copy. Dunford's five components (built in order, each depending on the last):

1. **Competitive alternatives** — what the customer would do if you didn't exist (often a spreadsheet
   or nothing, not a named rival).
2. **Unique attributes** — what you have that the alternatives don't.
3. **Value** — the benefit those attributes deliver that the customer cares about.
4. **Target customers** — the buyers who care most about that value.
5. **Market category** — the frame you put yourself in so the value is obvious at a glance.

(The book adds an optional sixth: a relevant trend, used carefully.)

**How to apply.** Before writing a landing page or buying a channel, work the five components in order
from the customers' actual alternatives up to the category you claim. The right category is the one
that makes your unique value *obvious* to your best-fit buyer — not necessarily the biggest or the
trendiest. Then let message, audience, and channel follow from that frame.

**When it applies.** Any acquisition surface that is converting poorly despite traffic, a product
being compared to the wrong things, or a launch into a crowded or unclear category. Feeds
[[acq-principle-message-market-fit]] (the message) and [[acq-framework-beachhead-narrow-icp]] (the
target).

**Caution (honesty).** Positioning is a judgement call validated by whether best-fit buyers "get it"
faster and convert (`signup_rate`), not a provable fact — test it, don't declare it. Claiming a
category you can't credibly hold backfires. Keep comparative claims about alternatives truthful;
inflating or misrepresenting them is a marketing-ethics line, not just a weak tactic.
