**Principle — the message must channel demand the audience already has, not try to create it.**

Schwartz's core idea: copy cannot manufacture desire — it can only take the hopes, fears, and wants
that already exist in a market and focus them onto your product. So acquisition messaging works when it
**names a demand the audience already feels** and connects it to what you offer; it fails when it tries
to talk people into wanting something they don't. This is "message-market fit": the match between what
you say on the acquisition surface and what the target already wants. It sits downstream of positioning
(which decides the frame and the target) and shows up directly in `signup_rate` — the right message to
the right audience converts visitors; a clever message to the wrong audience, or a generic one to
everyone, does not.

**How to apply.** Start from the demand your best-fit segment already has (from customer language, not
your feature list) and lead with that, then show how the product channels it. Keep the ad→landing-page
scent consistent so the message a visitor clicked is the message they land on. When `signup_rate` is
low despite real traffic, suspect message-market fit (or positioning) before blaming the channel.

**When it applies.** Writing or diagnosing any acquisition message — ad, landing hero, outbound hook —
especially a high-traffic/low-signup surface. Pairs with [[acq-framework-positioning-dunford]] (sets
the frame) and [[acq-framework-beachhead-narrow-icp]] (a narrow ICP has a sharper existing demand to
channel). The *page/flow mechanics* of converting that message live in the acquisition-conversion
family (built later).

**Caution (ethics).** "Channel existing desire" is not licence to exploit it. Fear-based or
insecurity-based hooks, fake urgency, and promising a desire the product doesn't actually satisfy are
deceptive-marketing anti-patterns — never prescribed; several breach advertising / consumer-protection
law. For regulated archetypes (bank/wallet, betting) messaging that channels financial or gambling
desire carries a heightened responsible-use / financial-promotion compliance caution. The message must
be one the product can honestly deliver on.
