**Principle — remind lapsing users what they'll lose, not just what they'd gain (loss aversion).**

Loss looms larger than an equivalent gain. Evidence distilled from the book: Tversky & Kahneman
(1979) — on a coin-flip bet, most people wouldn't risk losing **$10** unless they could win at least
**$20** (~2×). Gonzales et al. (1988) — homeowners told they would **save** $0.75/day by insulating
took it up **39%** of the time; those told they would **lose** $0.75/day took it up **61%**. Same
economics, different frame.

**How to apply (win-back / churn-prevention).** Frame re-engagement around **what the user will lose**
— their saved work, their streak, their accumulated benefits, their access — not only what they'd
gain by returning. The feeling of deprivation ("Got Milk?") is a stronger pull than a reminder of
upside.

**When it applies.** Win-back of lapsing users, churn-prevention, renewal moments.

**Caution (ethics).** The loss must be **real** — genuine value the user would actually forfeit.
Never fabricate loss, invent false scarcity, or manufacture urgency ("your account will be deleted
in 24h!") — those are dark patterns, documented not prescribed. For bank/wallet and betting, never
use loss-framing to push deposits or wagering (loss-chasing — responsible-gambling caution). Pairs
with [[ret-principle-stored-value-switching-costs]] and [[ret-tactic-reengagement-lifecycle]].
