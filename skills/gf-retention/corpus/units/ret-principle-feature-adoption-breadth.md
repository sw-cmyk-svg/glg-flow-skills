**Principle — users who adopt more of the core features retain better (breadth of use).**

Retention tracks how much of a product's value a user actually uses. In the Pendo benchmark, the
stickiest industries (Manufacturing, Consumer Goods ~55% WAU/MAU) also had the **highest feature
adoption and retention** — "as users get more value from features, they're more likely to keep
returning." Breadth of core-feature adoption is a leading indicator of whether a user sticks.

**How to apply.** Identify the handful of core features that correlate with retention on the client's
own data (the "habit features"), then move users toward adopting **more of them over time**, not just
the first one. Depth of use, not only return frequency, is the lever.

**When it applies.** Multi-feature products where value compounds across features. Less so for
single-purpose tools.

**Caution (causal honesty).** This is **correlational** — feature adoption and retention move
together, but adopting a feature doesn't prove it *causes* retention (power users adopt more of
everything). Find the client's own retention-correlated features on their data rather than assuming
a feature drives it. Pairs with [[ret-benchmark-stickiness-pendo]] and
[[ret-tactic-guide-to-value-continuously]].
