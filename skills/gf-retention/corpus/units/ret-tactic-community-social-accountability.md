**Tactic — belonging and social accountability give users a reason to keep showing up.**

People return for other people. A community (forum, shared feed, cohort, leaderboard, visible
progress among peers) adds a social reason to come back that the core feature alone doesn't provide —
social proof pulls behaviour toward what peers do, and gentle accountability (others can see you
showed up) sustains a habit. For some products the community becomes a bigger retention driver than
the product itself.

**How to apply.** Offer a genuine place for users to connect around the job they're doing — peer
help, shared progress, recognition. Keep it useful and well-moderated. Where you use visible progress
or peer comparison, frame it as encouragement, not pressure, and let users opt out of being seen.

**When it applies.** Products with a shared practice, learning curve, or goal where peers add value
(learning tools, creator tools, fitness/habit, developer tools).

**Caution (ethics) — real here.** Social accountability slides into manipulation fast: public
shaming, guilt mechanics ("you're falling behind everyone"), manufactured competition, and streak/loss
pressure ([[ret-tactic-variable-rewards-habit]], [[ret-principle-loss-aversion-winback]]) are dark
patterns — documented, never prescribed. Comparison can harm wellbeing; keep it opt-in and kind. For
bank/wallet and betting, never use social pressure to drive spending or wagering (responsible-use
caution). Pairs with [[ret-principle-network-effects-retention]].
