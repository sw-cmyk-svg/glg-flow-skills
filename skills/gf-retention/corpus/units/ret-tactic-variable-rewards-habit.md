**Tactic — variable rewards pull users back (handle with the most care in the corpus).**

An *unpredictable* reward pulls return harder than a predictable one. From the book: Zeiler (1972)
pigeons pecked **~2×** as often when the pellet came unpredictably; Mazar et al. (2017) sold **+43%**
under a 1-in-3 chance of free vs an equal-value fixed discount. The Facebook example: you post and
don't know how many Likes you'll get, so you **keep checking** — the uncertainty, tied to dopamine
anticipation, is the pull. It is the "variable reward" phase of [[ret-framework-hook-model]].

**How to apply (lightly).** A *genuine* element of surprise-and-delight on return — fresh relevant
content, an unexpected useful update — can strengthen a habit more than a flat, predictable one.

**When it applies / honest limit.** Products with a natural return cadence. **Too little reward
backfires** (Zeiler: birds disengaged at 10–30% reward frequency) — a mostly-nothing loop
*demotivates*.

**Caution (ethics) — the strongest in the corpus.** This is the engine of compulsion. Keep any use
mild, genuine, and non-compulsive; never build a variable-reward loop engineered to maximise
time-on-app against the user's wellbeing (a dark pattern — documented, never prescribed). For
bank/wallet and betting, variable-reward / regret-lottery mechanics around deposits, spending, or
wagering carry a heightened caution advising regulatory and responsible-gambling compliance. Mirrors
the activation corpus (`act-tactic-uncertain-rewards`) and `copilot/GUARDRAILS.md`.
