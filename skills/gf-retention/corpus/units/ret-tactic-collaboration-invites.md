**Tactic — get a second person into the workspace; collaboration is retention.**

Inviting a teammate, sharing a workspace, or starting a collaborative artifact turns a solo tool into
shared infrastructure. In Hook terms ([[ret-framework-hook-model]]), the invite is **investment** —
the user loads value in (other people, shared work) that makes the product harder to walk away from
and better the next time they return. "Multiplayer" accounts and shared workspaces routinely retain
and expand better than single-seat usage.

**How to apply.** Make inviting collaborators a natural step on the path to value, at the moment
sharing is genuinely useful (not a signup-wall gate). Lower the friction for the invitee to get value
fast (they're a new user too — the activation rules apply to them). Measure retention and expansion of
accounts with 2+ active users vs solo accounts.

**When it applies.** Products where the real work is shared — docs, projects, data, pipelines,
dashboards — or where a second user unlocks a distinct benefit.

**Caution (ethics).** Invites must be **wanted and consented** — don't auto-import or spam the user's
contacts, don't send invites on their behalf without clear intent, and honour the invitee's choice.
Forced-invite walls and contact-harvesting are dark patterns (documented, not prescribed), and they
breach anti-spam / privacy rules. Pairs with [[ret-principle-network-effects-retention]] and
[[ret-tactic-reengagement-lifecycle]].
