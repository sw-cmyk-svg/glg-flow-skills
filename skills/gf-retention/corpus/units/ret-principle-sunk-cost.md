**Principle — prior investment keeps users engaged (sunk cost).**

People keep using what they've already paid for or put effort into, to justify the investment, even
when it isn't rational. Evidence distilled from the book:
- **Arkes & Blumer (1985):** theatre season-ticket holders who paid the full **$15** attended
  **4.11** shows vs **3.29** for those who paid **$8** — **~25% more**, because they had the highest
  sunk cost.
- **Amazon Prime:** members spend **~$110/mo** vs **~$38** for non-members (partly self-selection,
  the book notes).
- **The authors' Kantar study:** imagining a paid Prime subscription raised willingness to pay the
  Amazon premium from **37% to 40%** (+8%) — the pull of not "wasting" the fee.

**How to apply (ethically).** Genuine prior investment aids retention — an annual plan, time spent
setting up, data imported. Make that investment **real and visible** (remind users what they've
built or earned) so continuing feels worth it.

**When it applies.** Products with upfront commitment (annual plans, setup effort, imported data).

**Caution (ethics).** Use **genuine** sunk cost (real value the user chose to invest); never
manufacture fake sunk costs or make leaving artificially painful (hard-to-cancel, forced
continuity) — those are dark patterns, documented not prescribed. For bank/wallet and betting, never
exploit sunk cost to keep users spending or wagering (responsible-use / gambling caution). Pairs with
[[ret-principle-stored-value-switching-costs]].
