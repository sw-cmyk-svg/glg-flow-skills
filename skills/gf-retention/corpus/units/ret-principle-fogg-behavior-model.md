**Principle — a behaviour happens only when motivation, ability, and a prompt meet (Fogg, B=MAP).**

**B = MAP:** a behaviour (e.g. returning to the product) occurs only when **Motivation**, **Ability**,
and a **Prompt** converge at the same moment. When the behaviour doesn't happen, at least one of the
three is missing:
- **Motivation** — do they want to? (fluctuates, hard to sustain)
- **Ability** — is it easy? (**usually the better lever** — make returning effortless)
- **Prompt** — is there a timely cue? (a notification / email at the right moment)
Three prompt types by what's lacking: **Spark** (low motivation, high ability — a prompt that also
motivates), **Facilitator** (high motivation, low ability — a prompt that makes it easier), **Signal**
(both high — a simple reminder).

**How to apply (Diagnose).** When a return isn't happening, ask **which of the three is missing**.
Often it's **ability** (too hard to get back to value) or **prompt** (no timely cue), not motivation
— so make re-entry easy and cue it well before trying to pump motivation.

**When it applies.** Diagnosing any desired repeat behaviour (return, re-engage, adopt a feature).

**Caution (ethics).** Prompts must respect **consent and frequency** — a well-timed cue helps;
relentless prompts are spam and manipulation (see [[ret-tactic-reengagement-lifecycle]]).
