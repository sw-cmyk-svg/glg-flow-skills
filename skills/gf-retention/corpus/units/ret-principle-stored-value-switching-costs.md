**Principle — accumulated, earned value makes a product stickier (and leaving costlier).**

As users put work into a product — data, content, settings, connections, history — it becomes more
valuable to them and harder to leave. This is the Hook model's **investment** phase
([[ret-framework-hook-model]]) and rests on the **endowment / IKEA effect**: we value what we've
built more than what we're handed.

**How to apply.** Design the product so ordinary use **accumulates visible value**: saved work, a
growing library, a configured workspace, a usage history. Surface it ("you've saved 40 items",
"your workspace has 12 dashboards"). Switching costs that come from **real accumulated value** are
the ethical, durable form of retention — the user stays because leaving means losing something they
actually built.

**When it applies.** Products where use builds up state (notes, designs, data, networks). Less so for
stateless or purely transactional tools.

**Caution (ethics).** The line is **earned vs trapped**. Real accumulated value the user can export
and benefit from is good; artificial lock-in (hostage data, no export, deliberately painful
migration) is a dark pattern — documented, never prescribed, and it also cuts against HR1's
survive-our-disappearance spirit. The endowment/IKEA framing is established behavioral science cited
generally — confirm the specific study before quoting a figure. Pairs with [[ret-principle-sunk-cost]].
