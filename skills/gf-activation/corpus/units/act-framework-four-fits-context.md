**Framework — is it really an activation problem? (Balfour's Four Fits as a sanity check.)**

Balfour's Four Fits says a durable growth business needs all of: **Market↔Product**,
**Product↔Channel**, **Channel↔Model**, **Model↔Market** — and that they form an ecosystem that
keeps changing, so none should be judged in isolation. Activation work lives inside
**Market↔Product Fit**: it's how new users reach the value the product-for-this-market promises.

**Why it matters here.** It's a **diagnostic guardrail**: if users activate but don't stick
(retention curves never flatten), the real problem is upstream — product or market fit — and
polishing onboarding is **polishing a leaky bucket**. Conversely, strong retention among the few
who activate says the bottleneck genuinely *is* activation, and the specialist's playbook
applies.

**How to apply (Diagnose).** Before prescribing activation tactics, check the shape of the funnel
(see [[act-framework-locate-the-problem]]): many sign up but few activate → activation problem;
many activate but few retain → a fit/value problem no onboarding tweak will fix.

**When it applies.** The Diagnose step, and any time a client asks to "improve onboarding" before
confirming where the leak is.

**Caution.** Four Fits is a **high-level strategy frame**, not an activation playbook; use it to
*locate* the problem, not to size tactics. The retention-curve test is from Balfour's broader
writing, not the single Four Fits page. Pairs with
[[act-benchmark-activation-predicts-retention]].
