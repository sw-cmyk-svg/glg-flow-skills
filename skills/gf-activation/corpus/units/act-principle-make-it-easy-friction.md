**Principle — even tiny friction stops progress ("make it easy").**

Small frictions have outsized effects on whether people continue. In the cited study,
inserting a trivial interruption — dyeing every 7th chip in a tube — cut consumption from
**45 to 20 chips (~56% less)**; every 5th chip cut it 59%. A single extra click, field, or
decision has the same braking effect on a user mid-flow.

**How to apply.** Walk the whole signup→first-value path and remove every bump: each extra
field, click, confirmation, or decision point. "One more step" is never free.

**When it applies.** Universal for activation. The rare exception is deliberate friction
that protects the user (security / KYC confirmations) — keep those, cut the rest.
