**Tactic — start the user partway, not at zero (endowed progress).**

People work harder toward a goal they've already begun. In Nunes & Drèze's car-wash field
experiment, a loyalty card that **required the same 8 purchases** but was framed as a 10-stamp
card with **2 stamps pre-filled** reached **34% completion vs 19%** for an empty 8-stamp card.
Applied to onboarding: a setup checklist that opens with the signup step already **ticked**,
and a progress bar that starts **above 0%**, pull users toward finishing.

**How to apply.** Make the activation checklist begin partially complete — credit what the
user has already done (account created, email verified). Show a progress indicator that starts
with real, earned progress, and sequence remaining steps toward first value.

**When it applies.** Multi-step setup or onboarding checklists. Low value for one-step
activation, where there is no "progress" to endow.

**Caution.** The figures are from a **retail loyalty-card** study (secondary-sourced);
treat the *mechanism* as transferable but the exact lift as illustrative, not a SaaS benchmark.
Needs a real multi-step path to work.

**Caution (ethics).** The endowed progress must reflect **genuine** steps toward **genuine**
value — crediting work the user actually did. Faking progress, inflating the bar, or dangling
rewards to push an action against the user's interest is a dark pattern (document, don't
prescribe). For bank/wallet and other financial archetypes, take care with progress/reward
mechanics around deposits, wagering, or risk-taking — advise the client to confirm regulatory and
responsible-gambling compliance. Pairs with
[[act-principle-goal-dilution]] (one goal) and [[act-principle-make-it-easy-friction]].
