**Case study — ASL Bloom: move social proof off the paywall and into onboarding.**

ASL Bloom (a sign-language learning app) originally showed social proof **only on the paywall**,
as testimonials. The redesign added **onboarding screens presenting social proof from two
perspectives — teachers and students** — and reinforced trust via expert-led learning (native
Deaf educators). The design lesson: surface social proof **early**, where new users first weigh
whether this product works for people like them, and from the **perspectives that matter to your
audience** — not only at the moment of payment.

**How to apply.** Put relevant, specific social proof at the first-run and empty states, not just
the paywall; match the proof to the user's identity (peer + authority).

**When it applies.** Signup → first-value, especially where trust/credibility gates adoption
(education, health, fintech).

**Caution (causal honesty).** The redesign's reported results — **+15% trial conversion, +12%
ARPU, +28% first-goal completion, +18% day-30 retention** — are for the **whole onboarding
overhaul** (also goal-based personalization, aspiration messaging, a reframed paywall). Social
proof's isolated contribution is **not** separated out, and the article doesn't state an A/B
design — so treat these as *redesign* outcomes, not proof that social proof alone drives them.

**Caution (ethics).** Testimonials and perspectives must be **real**. Pairs with
[[act-principle-social-proof]] and [[act-principle-implied-social-proof]].
