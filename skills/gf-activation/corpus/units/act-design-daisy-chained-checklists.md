**Case study — Appcues: short, staged, unlocking checklists (completion 2% → 25%).**

Appcues replaced one long onboarding checklist with a **daisy-chained** pair:
- **Checklist 1:** a *single* task — install the Appcues Builder.
- **Checklist 2:** **unlocks only after Checklist 1 is done**, then shows three items (create a
  flow, track an event, create a goal).

Chunking the work so users see only a few small tasks at a time raised **checklist completion
from 2% to 25%**.

**The design lesson.** Don't show a long to-do list. Break activation into a couple of short
stages where finishing one **unlocks** the next — this is the applied, SaaS-native form of
[[act-tactic-endowed-progress]] (momentum from visible, staged progress) and
[[act-principle-goal-dilution]] (one goal at a time). A single-task first checklist removes the
"wall of work" that stalls new users.

**When it applies.** Any multi-step setup. Pair with a progress indicator (see
[[act-benchmark-product-tour-engagement]], where progress indicators lift completion ~12%).

**Caution.** Cite only the **2% → 25% completion** figure — the source's separate "activation
−20%" line reads as a wording error, is presented as a win, and is **not** attributed to the
checklist, so it is excluded here. Completion ≠ the spec's `activation_rate`; confirm the
downstream activation effect on the client's own funnel.
