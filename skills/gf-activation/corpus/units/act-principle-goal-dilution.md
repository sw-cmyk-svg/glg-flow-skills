**Principle — one goal per onboarding; extra goals dilute the one that matters.**

The *goal dilution effect*: people judge something as *less* effective at a goal when it is
pitched as achieving many goals than when it focuses on one. In the cited study, people
rated tomatoes as **12% more effective** at preventing cancer when that was the only stated
benefit than when a second benefit was added alongside it.

**How to apply.** Point onboarding at a single primary first-value action. Every extra
"while you're here" ask (invite teammates, connect a source, configure settings) dilutes
completion of the one that actually drives activation. Defer the rest until after the aha.

**When it applies.** Strong for the first session. Genuinely prerequisite multi-step setup
is the exception — but *sequence* it, don't present it all at once.
