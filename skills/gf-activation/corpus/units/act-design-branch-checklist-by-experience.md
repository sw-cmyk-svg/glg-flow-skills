**Case study — Hotjar: one branching question lifts the critical first step (+26% installs).**

Hotjar was losing users between account creation and install. They replaced a single generic
checklist with **experience-branched** ones: a single question — *"Have you used Hotjar
before?"* — routed users to a **Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced** checklist. The **first step
was identical in all three (install Hotjar on your site)**; only later steps varied. Users who
answered the question were **26% more likely to complete the install step**, and Hotjar baked
the question into its native onboarding.

**The design lesson.** You don't need deep personalization to move activation — a single
self-reported question, used to branch *pacing and later steps* while keeping the critical first
action constant, can lift completion of that action. This is the SaaS-native version of
[[act-tactic-segment-at-signup]]: the answer must *change what the user does next*.

**When it applies.** Products with a hard first step (install, connect, import) and a mix of
new vs experienced users.

**Caution.** The 26% is a **relative lift only** — the page gives no absolute before/after,
sample size, test duration, or significance, and doesn't fully define the comparison group.
Treat as directional. Pairs with [[act-design-daisy-chained-checklists]] and
[[act-principle-make-it-easy-friction]].
