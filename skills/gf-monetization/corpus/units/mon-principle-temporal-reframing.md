**Principle — a cost split into small, frequent units feels smaller than the same cost stated as a lump (temporal reframing / pennies-a-day).**

Breaking a price into a daily or monthly figure lowers the felt sacrifice. Evidence distilled from the
book: Gourville (Harvard, 1998) showed 120 people a donation framed as either **"$1 a day"** or
**"$350 a year"** (actually $15 *cheaper*); people were **53% more likely** to agree to the daily
framing. The explanation: a dollar conjures one small thing (a can of soda), while $350 conjures a
coat or a night away — a bigger imagined sacrifice.

**How to apply (SaaS display).** Present the price at the smallest honest cadence that fits the buyer —
"$12/month" rather than "$144/year," or "about $0.40 a day" for a prosumer plan — while still showing
the real total clearly. Useful for framing an annual plan's monthly-equivalent. It changes the
**framing**, not the price.

**When it applies.** Pricing-page display, annual-plan framing, lower-priced self-serve tiers.

**Caution (ethics + honesty).** The full amount and the real billing terms must stay **clearly
visible** — "$0.40/day, billed $144/year" is honest; showing only the daily figure to disguise a large
annual charge is drip-pricing/obfuscation, a dark pattern and often unlawful. Pairs with
[[mon-principle-present-bias-deferral]].
