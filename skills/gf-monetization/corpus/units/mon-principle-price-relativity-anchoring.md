**Principle — people judge a price against what sits next to it, not in the abstract (price relativity).**

The same price looks like good or bad value depending on its comparison set. Evidence distilled from
the book: shown a $3.99 tub of Ben & Jerry's next to a **cheaper** $1.99 own-label, only **27%** called
it great value; shown next to a **pricier** $4.99 Halo Top, **41%** did — a **52% swing** from the
comparator alone. In another test, a beer shown beside wines drew a willingness-to-pay **28% higher**
than when shown beside other beers. Red Bull's novel can size removed the soda comparison and let it
charge ~$2.50 vs ~$1 for Coke.

**How to apply (SaaS tiering).** This is why **tier structure is a pricing lever**, not just a
packaging one. A higher-priced tier reframes the tier below it as the sensible, good-value choice
(good-better-best; the middle tier benefits from being flanked). Order and present tiers so the one
you want most people to pick reads as the reasonable middle, and make the anchor a **real** option, not
a phantom.

**When it applies.** Any multi-tier pricing page; add-ons shown next to a base plan; enterprise anchor
above self-serve.

**Caution (ethics).** A **decoy** tier designed only to mislead — priced so no one would ever pick it,
purely to push another — edges toward manipulation; keep every tier a genuine, buyable option that
some segment actually wants. Pairs with [[mon-principle-charm-pricing]] and the packaging family.
