**Principle — three tiers (good-better-best) convert better than one price or a long menu.**

A small ladder of plans lets different segments self-select and gives the buyer a frame to judge value.
Three tiers is the common sweet spot: a **good** entry plan lowers the barrier, a **best** plan anchors
the top and makes the middle look reasonable (this is price relativity at work —
[[mon-principle-price-relativity-anchoring]]), and the **better** middle tier is usually where you want
most buyers to land. One price gives the buyer nothing to compare against; a long menu causes choice
paralysis.

**How to apply.** Build three tiers around the value metric: each step up should unlock clearly more of
the value the customer came for, not a random feature grab-bag. Design the middle tier as the intended
default and make its value obvious. Keep the number of tiers small (roughly 3, plus an enterprise
"contact us" where relevant). Name tiers by who they're for, not by internal jargon.

**When it applies.** Any self-serve pricing page; re-packaging a flat-priced product.

**Caution (ethics).** Tiers must be **honest segments** — each a plan some real group genuinely wants,
not a maze designed to confuse. Don't bury the thing most users need in a higher tier purely to force
upgrades (feature-gating against the user's core job is a grey-zone dark pattern). Pairs with
[[mon-tactic-feature-gating]] and [[mon-principle-decoy-tier]].
