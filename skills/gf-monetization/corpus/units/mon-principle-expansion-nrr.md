**Principle — durable SaaS growth comes from existing customers expanding (NRR > 100%).**

Net revenue retention (NRR) is revenue from your current customers this period vs last — expansion
(upgrades, more seats, more usage) minus downgrades and churn. When **NRR exceeds 100%**, the customer
base grows revenue even with zero new logos: the clearest sign of durable monetization. Expansion is
usually cheaper and higher-margin than new-customer acquisition, which is why it's the lever that
compounds. Usage-based and hybrid pricing tend to expand best, because the bill grows automatically as
the customer gets more value ([[mon-principle-usage-vs-seat]]).

**How to apply.** Build an expansion path into the product and the pricing: a value metric that grows
with the customer, upgrade triggers at real value moments, and seat/usage limits that a growing
customer naturally crosses. Measure NRR by cohort; if it's below 100%, find whether the leak is
downgrades, churn, or simply no path to spend more.

**When it applies.** Any product past early traction with a base worth expanding; especially PLG
land-and-expand.

**Caution (honesty + seam).** NRR blends expansion, downgrade, and **revenue churn** — a healthy
headline NRR can hide bad logo churn; read them separately. Expansion that comes from forced upgrades or
removing value from a lower tier is extraction, not growth, and backfires. The *usage* a customer gets
is Retention's; the *revenue* from it is this specialist's. Pairs with [[mon-benchmark-nrr-saas]] and
[[mon-tactic-upsell-at-value-moments]].
