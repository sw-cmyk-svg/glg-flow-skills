**Principle — deferring the moment of payment lowers price sensitivity and lifts conversion (present bias).**

People weight a cost now far more heavily than the same cost later — so moving the pain of payment
back makes buyers less price-sensitive and more likely to commit. Evidence distilled from the book:
Bain found almost **60%** of shops offering a "buy now, pay later" option saw a conversion lift, and
**47%** saw higher average order value; Klarna built a $15bn business on deferral. Present bias is a
long-established effect (Delaney & Lades, Stirling, 2017, 144 participants).

**How to apply (SaaS).** This is the honest logic behind the **free trial** and the **reverse trial**
(full access first, pay later) and behind "start now, we'll bill at renewal" — letting the user get
value before the cost lands reduces the commitment hurdle at the point of decision. It's a reason trials
and delayed first-charge often convert better than pay-upfront, when the product earns the trust in
between.

**When it applies.** Free-to-paid conversion, trial design, annual-plan first-charge timing.

**Caution (ethics) — real here.** Deferral works by muting the felt cost, so it can push people to
**overcommit or overspend**. Make the eventual charge, its date, and how to cancel **clear and easy** —
surprise renewals and hard-to-cancel flows are dark patterns (and, for auto-renewals, increasingly
unlawful: click-to-cancel rules). For bank/wallet and BNPL-style products, this carries a
**heightened responsible-lending / affordability caution** — never use deferral to drive borrowing a
user can't afford. Pairs with [[mon-principle-temporal-reframing]].
