**Tactic — offer a smaller plan or a pause before you accept a cancel; win back on real value.**

A customer cancelling a paid plan is often not rejecting the product, just the current price or a lull.
A **downgrade** (a cheaper tier) or a **pause** (keep the data, stop billing for a month or two) keeps
the relationship and the revenue optionality instead of losing both. Frame win-back around what they'd
**lose** — their saved work, history, settings ([[ret-principle-loss-aversion-winback]]) — when that
loss is real. A paused customer who returns is far cheaper to keep than a churned one to reacquire.

**How to apply.** In the cancel flow, offer the genuinely useful alternative that matches the stated
reason — too expensive → downgrade / annual discount; not using it right now → pause; missing a feature
→ route to the roadmap or a better-fit tier. For lapsed payers, a targeted win-back pointed at real
restored value beats a blanket discount. Measure saves and whether saved customers actually stay.

**When it applies.** Cancel flows, downgrade prevention, win-back of lapsed paying customers.

**Caution (ethics) — this is where cancel dark patterns live.** The cancel path must stay **easy and
honest** — a save offer is fine; burying the cancel button, forcing a call, guilt screens, or endless
"are you sure" loops are dark patterns and increasingly unlawful (click-to-cancel / auto-renewal laws).
Never fabricate the loss. For bank/wallet and betting, win-back must never nudge spending or wagering.
Pairs with [[mon-tactic-dunning-recovery]] and [[mon-principle-present-bias-deferral]].
