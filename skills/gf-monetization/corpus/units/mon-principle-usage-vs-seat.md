**Principle — seat-based pricing is simple; usage-based pricing expands better (pick for the product).**

Two dominant value metrics. **Seat-based** (per user) is simple, predictable, and easy to forecast —
good when value scales with headcount (e.g. Slack). **Usage-based** (per unit consumed) charges for
what's actually used (e.g. Twilio, AWS) and tends to **land smaller and expand automatically** as the
customer grows, which is why usage-priced companies tend to post stronger net revenue retention. Poyar's
data: ~**38%** of companies now price at least partly on usage, up over time; the industry trend is
toward **hybrid** (a base subscription plus usage).

**How to apply.** Choose by how value accrues: if value tracks *people*, seats are honest and easy; if
value tracks *consumption or outcomes*, usage (or a hybrid: platform fee + usage) captures growth
without a renegotiation. Usage lets a customer start cheap and pay more only as they get more value —
good for PLG land-and-expand — but needs predictability (caps, alerts, dashboards) so bills don't shock.

**When it applies.** Pricing-model selection; re-pricing a seat-based product that's leaving expansion
on the table; AI/consumption products where seats don't track value.

**Caution (honesty).** Usage pricing can produce **bill shock** and anxiety that suppresses usage —
the opposite of the goal. Give the customer visibility and control. Don't switch a customer base to
usage purely to raise bills without a value story. Pairs with [[mon-principle-value-metric]] and
[[mon-principle-expansion-nrr]].
