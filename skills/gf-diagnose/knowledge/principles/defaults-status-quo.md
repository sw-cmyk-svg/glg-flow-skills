## Claim
People disproportionately stick with pre-selected options even when
switching is nearly free. One of the most reliable effects in the
behavioral literature (meta-analytic d ≈ 0.63–0.68), driven by effort,
implied endorsement, and reference-point shifts.

## Applicability — works when
- The default is genuinely reasonable for most users and reversal is cheap
- Users face the choice while cognitively loaded (onboarding, checkout)
- The chooser has no strong prior preference

## Backfires when
- The default is perceived as self-serving (dark pattern) — acceptance
  looks fine but trust and later retention pay for it
- High-stakes or irreversible choices trigger deliberation; the default
  gets ignored or resented
- Regulated contexts (privacy, payments) where pre-selection is restricted

## Growth applications
- Pre-select the recommended plan on the pricing page (paying_conversion)
- Default-on weekly digest at signup (w1_retention) — measure unsubscribe
- Pre-filled template on first project creation (time_to_activation)

## Practice stance
When defaults and persuasion copy compete for the same experiment slot,
test the default first — it's cheaper and better-evidenced. Always pair
the acceptance metric with a downstream retention metric to catch
dark-pattern backfire before celebrating.
