## Claim
A small, voluntary commitment increases follow-through on a larger,
related ask later — the original foot-in-the-door study found residents
who agreed to a small request (a window sign) were far more likely to
later agree to a large one (a yard sign) than residents approached cold.
The mechanism is people acting consistently with their prior
self-image once they've committed to it. Graded B: the direction is
well established and widely replicated in compliance research, but
effect sizes vary a great deal with how voluntary and self-relevant the
initial commitment felt.

## Applicability — works when
- The initial commitment is small, easy, and genuinely voluntary — the
  person chose it, wasn't defaulted or pressured into it
- The later ask is logically related to the first, so consistency has
  something to latch onto
- There's time between the two asks for the self-image shift to settle

## Backfires when
- The "commitment" is actually forced — a mandatory long signup form or
  a required goal-setting step before any value is delivered is friction
  wearing commitment's clothes, and it repels rather than binds, because
  the mechanism depends on the choice feeling free
- The two asks feel unrelated, so there's no consistency narrative to
  invoke
- The initial ask was so trivial it wasn't experienced as a real choice
  (a pre-checked box doesn't count — that's a default, not a commitment)

## Growth applications
- Let users opt into a small, specific goal during onboarding (rather
  than requiring it) before asking for account setup effort
  (activation_rate)
- A voluntary "invite one teammate" micro-ask early, followed later by
  a team-plan upsell that references the stated goal (expansion_signups)
- Optional public-facing commitment (a shared roadmap vote, a named
  use case) ahead of a renewal or upgrade conversation (paying_conversion)

## Practice stance
We only count something as a commitment lever if the user could have
said no without friction — anything gated or mandatory doesn't get
credited as this pattern, it gets audited as friction instead. When we
sequence a small ask before a big one, we make the connection between
them explicit in copy, because consistency has to be invoked, not just
hoped for.
