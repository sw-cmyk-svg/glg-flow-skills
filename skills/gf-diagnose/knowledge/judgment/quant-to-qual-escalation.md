## Claim
Keep slicing quantitative data while each new breakdown still generates a
testable hypothesis. The moment further breakdowns stop producing
hypotheses — because the open question is about user motivation or
perception that events can't capture — stop slicing and switch to
qualitative research (interviews, session replay, surveys).

## Applicability — works when
- A funnel or metric anomaly has been sliced by several breakdowns and each
  new cut still suggests a specific, testable next hypothesis — keep going
  quantitatively
- The remaining open question is a "why" about user intent or perception
  that no further event-level breakdown could answer even in principle
- Qualitative methods (interviews, replays, surveys) are available and can
  be run at a cost/speed justified by the size of the open question

## Backfires when
- Going qualitative too early — before cheaper, faster quantitative
  breakdowns that would have answered the question are exhausted — wastes
  the qualitative budget on a question quant could have closed
- Endless slicing when the answer is plainly a "why" question no further
  breakdown will resolve — this stalls the diagnosis waiting on a
  quantitative signal that doesn't exist

## Growth applications
- When activation_rate breakdowns by device, cohort, and channel stop
  suggesting new hypotheses, move to session replay or user interviews
  rather than requesting another cut
- When signup_rate drop-off by traffic source is fully explained
  quantitatively, don't commission user research for a question already
  answered
- Use "does this breakdown suggest a new testable hypothesis?" as the
  literal go/no-go gate before recommending qualitative research

## Practice stance
We treat "no further breakdown produces a hypothesis" as the explicit
trigger to switch modes — not a vague sense that we've "tried enough." We
push quantitative slicing until it's genuinely exhausted before spending on
qualitative research, and we don't let a stalled quantitative analysis
linger past that point either.
