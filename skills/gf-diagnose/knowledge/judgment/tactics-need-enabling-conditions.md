## Claim
The tactic is rarely the problem — off-the-shelf application without the
scaffolding that makes it work is. Gamification without a design framework
(e.g. Octalysis) underperforms; discounts/promos without segmentation,
clear KPIs, and strict ROI discipline give inconsistent results; push
notifications need a frequency/relevance balance to avoid fatigue. Referral
programs and social proof generally work well — because they're usually
applied with the enabling conditions intact. Never recommend a tactic
without naming its enabling conditions alongside it.

## Applicability — works when
- Evaluating why a well-known growth tactic underperformed for a client —
  check for the missing scaffolding (design framework, segmentation,
  measurement discipline, product fit) before concluding the tactic itself
  doesn't work
- Recommending a tactic to a client — pair the recommendation with its
  specific enabling conditions, not just the tactic name
- Comparing two engagements' results with the "same" tactic — the enabling
  conditions are usually the actual variable, not the tactic

## Backfires when
- "The conditions weren't met" becomes an unfalsifiable excuse trotted out
  after any tactic fails, regardless of whether the scaffolding was
  actually the issue — this needs to be checked, not assumed
- Assuming scaffolding guarantees success — a gamification layer built on a
  proper framework can still fail for reasons the framework doesn't cover
  (product-market fit, timing, audience)

## Growth applications
- Before recommending gamification to lift w1_retention, confirm a design
  framework (Octalysis or equivalent) is in place, not just point mechanics
- Before greenlighting a promo aimed at paying_conversion, confirm
  segmentation, a named KPI, and an ROI threshold exist — a promo without
  these is a coupon, not a growth lever
- Before scaling push notifications against arpu, confirm a
  frequency/relevance policy exists to prevent fatigue-driven unsubscribes

## Practice stance
We don't recommend a tactic without its enabling conditions attached, and we
don't accept "the tactic failed" as a diagnosis without checking whether the
scaffolding was ever in place. What we don't yet have is a reliable
pre-launch predictor of which tactic will succeed — the honest kernel is
understanding the specific behavior you're trying to influence and how the
tactic targets it, which is necessary but not sufficient as a checklist. We
won't manufacture a false-precision predictive framework we don't actually
use in practice.
